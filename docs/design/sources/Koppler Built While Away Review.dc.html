Built while away: review

All three are close. Notes: offer the link on apps only, and show written notes as text. Direction: keep the chevron, a little heavier, on app-to-app cables too. Names: wrap to two lines before cutting.

1

Notes on every card

Keep the note on every card, but only apps offer it at rest. Elsewhere the link shows when the card is picked, and a written note reads as text.

Is an always-offered link too much on small cards?Yes. Most queues, databases and outside systems never get a note, and 30px on each one adds up on a board. Show "Add note" in the foot when the card is picked or focused, and in its settings menu. Hover alone isn’t enough: it fails on touch and for keyboard users.
Should a written note read as text, editable on click?Yes. An open textarea looks like a form waiting for input. Written notes show as text under a "Note for the agent" label with a note icon, clamped to two lines; click opens the textarea in place.

Which way a link goes

Keep the chevron. The receiving socket’s shape already carries the connection test (fix 12), so it can’t also carry direction. Give the chevron the cable’s 2px weight, move it clear of the ring, and use it on every cable that has a direction, app to app included.

Chevron by the socket, or change the socket shape?The chevron. The socket’s inside shows the last connection test (empty, testing, works, unsure, fails), and its ring is the click target for testing again. A second job would make both harder to read.
Should a cable between two local apps show direction the same way?Yes. A call has a caller and a callee just as a publish has a sender. One rule is easier to learn: a chevron points into whoever receives. Database and tunnel links stay without one, since they go both ways.

Long card names

Wrap to two lines, then cut. A name is how you tell cards apart, and the end of a name ("(local)", "v2", "EU") is often the part that differs.

Ellipsis, or wrap to two lines?Wrap to two lines, and use an ellipsis only after the second. Most names fit on one line, so few cards grow. The tooltip keeps the full name.
